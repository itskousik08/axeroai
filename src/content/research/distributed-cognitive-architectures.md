## Abstract

As autonomous systems migrate from controlled laboratory settings to the unpredictable dynamics of industrial sectors, the limitations of centralized cognitive architectures become increasingly apparent. This paper presents a novel framework for **Distributed Cognitive Architectures (DCA)**, specifically designed to address latency and single-point-of-failure vulnerabilities in high-stakes environments.

## Methodology

Our research leverages a multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) approach where each agent maintains a localized world model while asynchronously synchronizing state representations with a global semantic layer.

1. **Local Perception Modules**: High-fidelity sensory processing at the edge.
2. **Semantic Synchronization**: Low-bandwidth state updates using sparse vector representations.
3. **Emergent Coordination**: Collective behavior emerging from individual goal-seeking under shared constraints.

## Results

Initial deployments of DCA within the Axero Zenthous testbed demonstrate a 40% reduction in coordination latency and a significantly higher resilience to individual node failure compared to traditional centralized models.

## Implications for the Future

The shift towards decentralized intelligence is not merely a technical optimization but a fundamental architectural evolution. By distributing cognition, we enable systems that are more robust, scalable, and inherently more compatible with the decentralized nature of the physical world.
